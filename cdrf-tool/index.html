CDRF: Comprehensive Dependency Resilience Framework

Prototype decision-support tool built from an empirical study of 786 npm, PyPI and Maven packages (data collected 21 November 2026).

Live data comes from deps.dev and OSV.dev. If the package is in the study dataset, its measured number of dependent packages is used; otherwise enter it (from the registry or ecosyste.ms), or the tool falls back to the deps.dev count for the latest version, which is a lower bound.

How the scores are calculated

C, criticality (0 to 1): the package's percentile rank, within its ecosystem, of the number of packages that depend on it. This measures how far a failure could spread.

A, abandonment likelihood (0 to 1): the probability that the package stops releasing within 24 months. It comes from a logistic regression trained on 553 packages that were active in November 2024 (cross-validated AUC 0.84). Inputs: releases in the last 12 months, months since the last release, age, total versions, dependents and GitHub stars. A package that is already dormant (no release for 24 months, archived or deprecated) gets A = 1.

V, vulnerability exposure (0 to 1): 0.5 if the current version has a known advisory, plus up to 0.3 for known-vulnerable packages in its resolved dependency tree, plus up to 0.2 for its history of advisories.

RRI, Resilience Risk Index (0 to 100): 100 × C × (0.6A + 0.4V). This is impact × likelihood.

Response: high C and high A: Support or fork; low C and high A: Migrate; high C and low A: Sustain; low C and low A: Accept and monitor. A known advisory in the current version, or in a direct dependency, adds a Remediate now flag whatever the quadrant.

Version 1.1 refinements (after the expert-panel review): R1: a deprecated package with high A is always Migrate, because deprecation usually names a successor. R2: a dormant, high-C package with no advisory history and no vulnerable dependencies is Sustain (watch), not Support or fork, because it may simply be finished.

Limits: the scores cannot predict account takeovers of actively maintained packages (e.g. chalk in September 2025), and "dormant" does not always mean "abandoned": some packages are simply finished.
